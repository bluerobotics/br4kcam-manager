# Blue Robotics 4K Cam — camera & lens facts (as of 2026-11-06)

Legend: [SPEC] = vendor document · [MEASURED] = measured on our cameras · [CODE] = from our code/protocol · [FIRMWARE] = from disassembling the camera firmware · [INFERRED] = derived, not directly confirmed

## Hardware
- Product: Blue Robotics "4K Cam", BR-103829 (motorized zoom/focus) [SPEC: https://bluerobotics.com/store/sensors-cameras/cameras/4k-cam/]
- Camera module: Shenzhen SMT (smtsec) SIP-K678K5 [SPEC: https://www.smtsec.com/productinfo/1212255.html]
  - SoC: HiSilicon Hi3519DV500 (dual Cortex-A55 @ 1 GHz, 2.54 TOPS)
  - Board supports: RS485, an auto-iris (DC-iris) lens connector (自动光圈接口), IR-cut, smart IR / white light (level or PWM), USB, UART, audio
  - Power: DC 12 V, ≤3 W (module); BR product: 5–55 V in, 5 W max
- Sensor: Sony IMX678 (STARVIS 2), 1/1.8", 3840×2160 (~8.29 MP), 2.0 µm pixels [SPEC]
  - Sensitivity: 14 815 digit/(lx·s) (IMX678-AAQR) / 16 309 (AAQR1), typ., 12-bit, Tj 60 °C [SPEC: Sony flyer https://www.sony-semicon.com/files/62/flyer_security/IMX678-AAQR_AAQR1_Flyer.pdf]
  - Saturation signal: 3 895 digit min, 12-bit [SPEC]
  - Gain: 0–30 dB analog (0.3 dB steps), then up to +42 dB digital (72 dB total) [SPEC]
  - Has high/low conversion-gain modes; which mode the camera uses is unknown
- Lens: 3.6–11 mm ±5 %, 2× zoom, F1.5 (at wide end; tele f-number unknown), HFOV 94° / VFOV 62°, focus 0.2 m–∞, D14 mount [SPEC: BR page]
  - BR page says "Iris: Automatic aperture" — but [MEASURED] sweeping auto_iris (0/1/2) and irisLevel (0/128/255) with exposure locked changed nothing. [FIRMWARE] auto_iris configures the SDK's DC iris (and the exposure minimum), irisLevel is never read, and no iris driver ships. Board has an auto-iris connector; whether this lens has an iris wired to it is unknown → ask hardware team.
  - "0.001 lux @ F1.2 (AGC on)" on the BR page is the module's marketing figure, not this lens.
- Focus/zoom/tilt: servo-driven via the autopilot (Navigator), not the camera API. Focus/zoom PWM 885–2250 µs; tilt servo Hitec HS-5055MG, 126° max range. [SPEC/CODE]
- Both test units (192.168.2.10 = bc071802-2c6d-8801-ae86-bc0718022c6d, 192.168.2.11 = bc071803-0839-8801-ae86-bc0718030839) are the same motorized camera; .10 is configured as "fixed focus" only for feature testing. (.11 previously announced bc071803-0839-8301-ac36-bc0718030839.)

## Streams [SPEC + MEASURED]
- rtsp://<camera>:554/stream_0 → main, 3840×2160 @ 30 fps (H.264 or H.265, configurable); stream_1 → substream, H.264/H.265/MJPEG (encode_type 1/5/3), up to 30 fps; bitrate silently capped at 8192 kbps [MEASURED]. MJPEG is non-standard: whole JPEGs inside RTP payloads, so GStreamer's decoders drop every frame (ffmpeg tolerates it); the manager's VencConf types lack encode_type 3. /action/live.flv (HTTP-FLV) is the main 4K H.264 stream; the web UI's snapshot buttons are browser-side, but the camera has its own still endpoint (see "Still capture" below).
- Command-to-frame delay (setting change → visible): 0.22–0.29 s with GStreamer on stream_1 [MEASURED]. Earlier 0.26–0.6 s figures were inflated by ffmpeg, which falls behind decoding 4K in software (minutes stale over long runs).
- Camera quirk [MEASURED]: a darkening change within ~2 s of a brightening one kills the current RTSP connection (new connections work); normal darkening steps pause frames 0.2–0.5 s. Image-setting writes can also drop the stream briefly at connect.

## PRO end-to-end on both firmwares [MEASURED, 2026-11-07, .10 V18 + .11 V16, main stream watched]
- Shutter priority (auto_exposureEx=1 + gain switches off), the max ISO caps (6400 → camera ISO 8630 exactly) and the 30 µs exposure floor with auto_iris off (1/10000 → 7 lines) work the same on V16 as on V18.
- Exposure jumps applied at once: 1/10000 → 1/30 (8 stops brighter) froze .11's (V16) main stream for 26 s without closing it; 1 s ISO 204800 → 1/30 ISO 800 (~10 stops darker) dropped its connection 0.9 s later. .10 (V18) took the same patterns.
- Ramped at ≤1 stop per 250 ms (PRO's slew limit), every ramp on both cameras (7-stop shutter and 11-stop ISO sweeps, brighten-then-darken ×3, EV, limits) streamed at ~30 fps with no gap over 150 ms. Switching into the camera's auto modes (Shutter, Program) pauses the stream once for 0.2–0.3 s.
- Entering Shutter priority from a very dark manual exposure (ISO 100, ~6 stops under) made V16's auto exposure raise gain at once and dropped the connection for 0.4 s (twice in two runs); V18 did not.
- Colour temperature: auto_awb=2 + wb_temp (1500–15000 K) gives manual WB gains from the ISP's calibration on both firmwares, smoothly (R/B at 2500 K 1.27/2.98 on .10, 1.15/3.13 on .11; at 10000 K 2.14/1.68 and 2.39/1.03). The two cameras differ, probably by awb_auto_mode (scene calibration set): .10 was on Blue, .11 on Green. /proc/umap/isp prints gains below 0x100 padded (`0x  df`). getISPInfo ColorTemp is the camera's estimate of the light and stays live in every WB mode (.11: 6172–6289 K alike in Auto and at wb_temp 3000 and 9000), so it does not follow wb_temp.

## Still capture: GET /action/cgi_images [MEASURED, 2026-11-06]
- Source: SMTSec (via Wilson). GET or POST, no auth. Always 3840×2160 (main channel); chn/channel/stream/width/height/quality are ignored. No header in the binary formats; Content-Type is wrong for all of them.
- No `fmt` (or any unknown value: jpg, png, rgb, nv12, dng…) → JPEG, ~150–175 kB, ~0.08 s.
- `fmt=yuv` → 12 441 600 B = YUV 4:2:0 semi-planar (Y plane, then interleaved chroma), 8-bit, full range (Y mean matches the JPEG's). Chroma order NV21 (V first): VPSS outputs YVU-SP420 (/proc/umap/vpss on .10). ~1.4 s. Works on both firmwares.
- `fmt=raw` → 16 588 800 B = 3840×2160 × uint16 little-endian, 12-bit data (clips at 4095; VI pipe pixel_format raw12), black level ≈ 300 (ISP black level 1200 on its 14-bit scale, all four channels), RGGB (/proc/umap/isp bayer=rggb, no flip/mirror). Same orientation as the JPEG. It is pre-ISP: sensor analog gain is in it, ISP digital gain (ISPDGain) is not. ~5.7 s per frame.
  - Firmware-dependent: works on .10 (18.010.U35.6_0054_V18(261030), platform v10.2.1); on .11 (18.010.U35.5_0054_V16(260305), platform v9.2.2) it answers `failed` instantly, HTTP 200. This is the "failed" Wilson saw; update the firmware to get raw.
- `fmt=bmp` → `failed` (on .11).

## Camera HTTP API [CODE]
- POST http://<camera>/action/<action> with JSON body, no auth (authenticated cgi_action variant doesn't work).
- Actions: getImageAdjustment / setImageAdjustment (base), getImageAdjustmentEx / setImageAdjustmentEx (advanced), getVencConf / setVencConf, getSysConfig, restart.
- The camera's own web UI sends one field per set request.
- Exposure values (exposure_time and max_exposure), from the camera's web UI: 1 (1 s), 2, 3, 6, 12, 25, 30, 50, 60, 100, 150, 200, 250, 300, 350, 400, 600, 800, 1000, 2000, 4000, 8000, 10000 (all = 1/x s), and 34464 = 1/100 000 s (100000 truncated to 16 bits; UI label "1/100000"). Module spec: shutter 1/12–1/100 000 s.

## Firmware knob map (V18) [FIRMWARE + LIVE, 2026-11-07]
How each JSON field reaches the HiSilicon ISP SDK, from disassembling firmware 18.010.U35.6_0054_V18 (lib/libplatform.so parses the JSON; soft/xipc, UPX-packed, makes the SDK calls) and checked on .10 by changing one field at a time and reading `/proc/umap/isp` over telnet. "LIVE ✔" = the proc state changed as the disassembly predicts. .11 runs V16, which was not disassembled.
- Set requests merge: the camera reads its current table, overwrites the fields sent, and applies only fields whose value changed (re-sending a value is a no-op) and whose bit is set in `validSupport` (others are dropped silently: ai_isp, antiDIS on our units). u8 fields wrap mod 256, no clamping; exposure_time/max_exposure are u32 in the request and truncated to u16 in xipc (hence 34464).
- The SDK clamps manual exposure and manual gains into the auto-exposure ranges. The firmware writes the gain ranges (auto_AGain_max, auto_DGain_max) in both modes, but the shutter range (max_exposure) only while applying auto exposure; that is why Manual's shutter is stuck at the max_exposure in force when Manual was entered.

## ISP readback: getISPInfo [MEASURED]
- POST /action/getISPInfo answers a text field, e.g. `ISP Info: ISO:2089 AGain:21394 DGain:1024 ISPDGain:1024 ExpTime:197 Exposure:4115 HistError:50 ColorTemp:5847 Saturation:105`. Fields can be missing from a read; parse by name.
- Gains are ×1024 fixed point; ISO = 100 × AGain × DGain × ISPDGain / 1024³ (ISO 100 = no gain, our convention too); Exposure = ExpTime × gain.
- manual_DGain is applied as ISPDGain = max(1, ⌊D/4⌋) × 1024: integer steps (D 8 → 2×, 10 → 2×, 13 → 3×, 160 → 40×), and only up to auto_DGain_max× (see Firmware knob map). DGain (sensor digital) stays 1×.
- manual_AGain A requests max(1, ⌊A/4⌋)×, capped at 0.75 × auto_AGain_max; AGain readback: 0 → 1.00×, 11 → 2.00×, 20 → 4.90×, 40 → 9.77×, 83 → 19.5×, 167 → 40.3×, 179 → 43.2×, 191 → 46.2×, 235 → 56.9×, ≥252 → 60.95× (max).
- ExpTime (µs) is live only in auto exposure (1/30 s frame-limited in dim light; 257 µs ≈ 1/3891 with max_exposure 1/4000). In manual it keeps auto exposure's last value, so it cannot read the manual shutter.
- Auto exposure applies more gain than the manual registers reach: AGain up to 75× (manual max 61×), ISPDGain up to 66×, ISO readback up to ~494 000 (manual max ~244 000).
- max_sys_gain read with the lens covered (auto exposure at its gain ceiling, 1/30 s; tools/iso_calibration/data/…_max_sys_gain_dark.csv): the ceiling is linear, camera ISO ≈ 4804 × value (≈48× gain per unit, analog first: 1 → 47.9× analog; 2 → 75× analog then digital), saturating at ~112–120 around camera ISO 535k–574k (75× analog × ~74× digital). From ~200 up it wobbles (485k–553k): auto exposure hunting at its limit.
- PRO uses this: `max_iso` → max_sys_gain 100 → 0, 6400 → 2, 12800 → 4, 25600 → 8, 51200 → 18, 102400 → 33, 204800 → 51, 409600 → 78, 819200 → 120 (full gain; the last two extrapolated). Verified live: max_iso 25600 wrote 8 and Program then read ISO 24826 at 1/30 s with the lens covered.
- Cross-check with the light-based ceilings, both converted to our light-calibrated ISO: dark readback is 0.06–0.58 stop higher (max_sys_gain 4: 13.3k vs 9.8k; 12: 35.9k vs 29.4k; 48: 187k vs 125k). Converting assumes brightness depends on total gain only; auto exposure splits it differently (more analog) than manual.
- Applied gain vs produced brightness: per light-calibrated stop the readback gain ratio is 2.00, 2.45, 1.99, 2.00, 2.06, 2.14, 2.14, 2.14, 2.14, 1.86, 1.54. They agree within ±0.04 stop through the analog mid-range, but the 200→400 step jumps +0.29 stop (coarse analog register) and the digital range drifts up to ~0.6 stop: at high gain the image brightens more than the gain alone explains (black level, noise floor or an enhancement stage).

## Measured image behavior (camera .11, white paper target)
- Exposure modes: full auto (auto_exposureEx=0) ignores the manual gains. **Shutter priority is native** [MEASURED, .10, 2026-11-07; the earlier "no priority" came from toggling auto_gain_mode, which the firmware ignores]: auto_exposureEx=1 + exposure_time + manual_AGain_enable=0 + manual_DGain_enable=0 → the shutter stays put (1/250 → 3998 µs, 1/1000 → 1007, 1/60 → 16659) and auto exposure drives analog + ISP digital gain to its target (HistError live; blc_level still sets the target: 20 dropped ISO 297k → 50k at 1/60). Its gain is capped exactly by auto_AGain_max/auto_DGain_max (1/250, dark: caps 32/4 → AGain 24× × ISPDGain 4×, ISO 9595, HistError 118 = underexposed; 255/0 → 75× analog only; 255/16 → 75 × 16×; 255/255 → ISO 1.9 M, 75× × 255×). max_sys_gain also pulls it down, but not as a clean total cap (4 with caps 255/255 → ISO 161k, 75× × 21×). No ISO priority: with auto_exposureEx≠0 the firmware always makes the shutter manual, and full auto pins the gain minimum at 1×.
- Analog gain (manual_AGain 0–255): no effect below ~8; coarse steps at low values (9–11 identical); then the gain about doubles with A (ISO stops at 11/20/40/83/167), working up to 255. Earlier "stops brightening above ~130" and "digital gain does nothing" were not the camera: those readings were identical to 0.01 across very different gains, the signature of the stale ffmpeg reader (see Streams).
- Digital gain (manual_DGain): works, at any max_sys_gain, but only up to auto_DGain_max× (with auto_DGain_max 0, as .10 had, it does nothing [MEASURED, .10]). Levels of 4 (0–7 no effect; 8–11 alike; 12–15 alike…) because the firmware applies ⌊D/4⌋×; multiplies with analog gain; D=8 ≈ 1.9× in brightness. Full A=255 D=252 ≈ 11.85 stops over no gain (~71 dB, spec 72 dB).
- max_sys_gain (camera UI "Max SysGain", 0–255): the ceiling on **auto exposure's** gain, applied live [MEASURED]. Auto exposure with the shutter capped at 1/4000 in dim light: 12 → luma 13, 64 → 49, 255 → 118.6 (its target). It does not limit manual gains, live or after booting with 12 (A=255 D=64 worked the same at 12 and 255). Default 12 on both cameras. Auto-exposure ISO ceiling per value [MEASURED, AE held to 1/4000 in dim light, each reading against manual ISO stops read right after; light steady to 0.08 stop; tools/iso_calibration/data/…_max_sys_gain.csv]: 0 → no gain; 1 → 4.6k; 2 → 4.8k; 3 → 9.3k; 4 → 9.8k; 6 → 16k; 8 → 24k; 12 → 29k; 16 → 39k; 24 → 60k; 32 → 83k; 48 → 125k; 64 → 206k; 80 → 355k; ≥96 → full gain (~369k). So stock auto exposure (12) stops ~3.6 stops short of full gain, and ceilings of ISO 200–3200 do not exist (any value above 0 allows ~4600). PRO Program mode exposes it as `max_iso`; MAX_ISO_GAINS in pro.rs now comes from the covered-lens readback below, not this light-based table. An earlier report that writing 255 stopped the stream did not recur (written live several times on .11).
- Exposure: halving the shutter darkens one stop from 1/50 to 1/4000. Exposures longer than a frame need a lower frame rate: at 30 fps, 1/25 is clamped (and froze the stream); at 15 fps on both channels, 1/25 adds about another stop over 1/50 and 1/12 a bit less than one more (clamped near the 1/15 frame time) [MEASURED, A=255: 1/50 69.5, 1/25 87.1, 1/12 97.0]. Changing frame_rate restarts the streams for a few seconds. 1/8000, 1/10000 and 34464 ("1/100 000") all expose identically, only ~half a stop below 1/4000 (≈1/5600 s) [MEASURED]. Cause [FIRMWARE + LIVE, .10]: auto_iris ≠ 0 (the default) sets the exposure minimum to 200 µs, which the sensor's 14.8 µs lines make 207 µs (≈1/4800). With auto_iris 0 the minimum is 30 µs: 1/8000 → 118 µs, 1/10000 → 104 µs, 34464 ("1/100 000") → 30 µs (2 lines, ≈1/33000). PRO turns auto_iris off on every mode switch and lists 1/30–1/10000 as calibrated manual shutters (max_shutter stays 1/30–1/4000).
- Leaving the camera's own image state (dehaze and 3D denoise switched off) shifts brightness ~15 % over ~20 s [MEASURED]; wait ~30 s before measuring.
- After every setting change the image creeps ~1.5 % for 6–8 s against the change, with the light steady [MEASURED]; noiseReduction is off, so the cause is unknown.
- 1/25 at 30 fps: stream stays 30 fps (exposure likely clamped) and froze on darkening steps; calibration avoids exposures longer than 1/50.
- blc_level (backlight compensation, 0–255, default 50) sets auto exposure's target brightness [MEASURED: 50 → luma ~122, 0 → ~43]: effectively exposure compensation. In EV, from the exposure auto exposure settled on (getISPInfo Exposure, dim light, shutter pinned at 1/30): 0 → −3.08, 10 → −1.31, 20 → −0.99, 30 → −0.55, 40 → −0.27, 50 → 0, 60 → +0.21, 70 → +0.41, 80 → +0.63, 100 → +1.04, 128 → +1.76, ≥160 → +2.00 (gain limit). PRO exposes it as `exposure_compensation`, −2 to +1.5 EV.
- auto_AGain_max and auto_DGain_max (0–255, Image tab sliders) cap auto exposure's analog and digital gain on top of max_sys_gain [MEASURED, both cameras, shutter capped at 1/4000 in dim light, max_sys_gain 120]: 32/0 (as .10 had) stops it at camera ISO 2398 (AGain 24×, no digital, luma 1–11); 255/32 → ISO 7.5k (AGain 75×); 255/255 → ISO 478–576k (luma ~106). .11 had 255/255 when the max_sys_gain tables were taken. PRO Program now writes both as 255. They also cap **manual** gains [MEASURED, .10, contradicts the earlier "auto only"]: with 32/0, manual A=167 D=40 read AGain 24× and ISPDGain 1×; opening auto_DGain_max gave 10×, and auto_DGain_max 8 cut D=160 to 8×. PRO now opens them on any mode switch or ISO change, Manual included.
- Slow shutter (low_farme_rate=1) works in auto exposure [MEASURED, .11, max_sys_gain 1 so gain runs out]: exposure 1/30 → 1/20 s, stream 31 → 21 fps, temporal noise 0.75 → 0.33, but the image ends ~10 % darker (luma 101 → 91) with auto exposure reporting it is on target (HistError 28 → 2). Repeatable over 25 s.
- Noise reduction does not change temporal noise at full gain [MEASURED, .11, ISO ~485k, flat target, substream, 25 s settle]: noiseReduction 0–3 and _2DNR_level 0–2 all read 2.88–2.97 (luma std over 90 frames). Readings taken within ~10 s of the change were off (up to 4.8): transients. Explained [FIRMWARE + LIVE, .10, V18]: both fields are stored and never applied; noise reduction follows ISO tables only.
- In that run auto exposure reached camera ISO ~1.22 M (blc_level ≥160), double the ~550 k ceiling seen with the lens covered: the covered-lens run did not find auto exposure's true top gain.
- Auto white balance gains cannot be read over HTTP: in Auto, getImageAdjustment's awb_red/green/blue keep the stored manual gains (unchanged by the scene), getISPInfo has only ColorTemp, and the camera's web UI has no other white-balance action. Over telnet, `/proc/umap/isp` shows them ("isp awb info" gain0–3). awb_style_red/green/blue are the web UI's "White balance style" sliders (0–255): a color bias, not a readout. It affects the image in both Auto and Manual white balance [OBSERVED by the user; size of the effect per step not measured]. The camera holds awb_auto_mode (the water scene) while in Manual.
- One-push white balance: writing onceAWB=1 directly to the camera changed neither auto_awb nor the RGB gains, in Auto or Manual (8 s watched). The manager's one-push path expects manual gains to move and settle; not reproduced by hand. V18 firmware: onceAWB runs its own closed loop on the video, not a copy of AWB's gains [FIRMWARE, xipc 472210; V16 has the same constants]. A thread polling every 50 ms (first check 5 s after start) picks up the request; the HTTP reply comes before any work, and a trigger during a run is dropped. Each step sets manual gains, waits 20 ms, grabs a VPSS chn1 frame, converts it to RGB with IVE and averages the centre 50 %×50 %. Blue water (awb_auto_mode 1): G and B fixed at 1×, R stepped (+4, or +16 when B−R ≥ 31; −4) until |R−B| ≤ 3, then R×1.2 (checked in the disassembly). Green water (anything else): G held at its start, R then B stepped toward G (±8, +60 for gaps ≥ 31). Only the first run after xipc starts switches AWB to auto and waits 500 ms (up to 300 steps); later runs start from the gains in use, manual ones included (up to 100 steps). The result is stored as awb_red = 0.95·(r−256)/15, awb_green/blue = (g−256)/8, truncated, with no clamping (gains below 1× underflow, high ones wrap), then re-applied, so the camera holds the re-quantised gains. Not saved to flash by this code. Crash path: a failed MMZ allocation per step is not handled and the pixel read goes through an uninitialised pointer (plausible, low). With G=B=1× by design in Blue water, a near-black view with a faint warm leak lands on 0/0/0. It works on V18 with a lit scene [user, 2026-11-07]. With a near-black scene (.10 pointed at a covered view) it lands on manual WB at 0/0/0, all gains 1× (ISP gains 0x100), instead of the auto gains (R 1.37×, B 2.40×), 5 of 5 writes [MEASURED, 2026-11-07]: nothing to measure, not a fault. One of those 5 was followed within ~10 s by an xipc segfault and a reboot (camera syslog `M_Common_Sigsegv`); the other 4 were not, and xipc had also segfaulted once earlier that day for an unknown reason, so the link is unproven. - max_exposure caps auto exposure's shutter, and in dim light auto exposure holds it there and raises gain [MEASURED, .11, full gain allowed]: cap 1/30 → 1/30 at ISO 8k, 1/250 → 1/250 at 72k, 1/1000 → 1/1000 at 252k, luma 119–125 throughout. - Manual shutter is clamped to the max_exposure in force when Manual was entered [MEASURED, .11]: entered with 1/1000, manual 1/30 and 1/250 both exposed 1/1000 (luma 7.9). - The camera keeps its image settings across a restart by itself [MEASURED, .10, /action/restart, back in ~21 s]: 8 changed fields over both tables (blc_level, max_exposure, max_sys_gain, auto_AGain_max, brightness, noiseReduction, low_farme_rate, _2DNR_level) all kept, also when restarted right after the writes; nothing else changed. A power cut was not tested. The manager never rewrites them at startup; it stores only actuator setup and positions, and PRO's shutter limit while in Manual.

## ISO calibration [MEASURED]
- Method: tools/iso_calibration/calibrate_iso.py (in PR bluerobotics/br4kcam-manager#306). Halve the shutter (exactly −1 stop) and find the gain that restores the reference brightness; known manual image state; H.264 substream via GStreamer, centered square; analog first, then the lowest digital level that reaches it, then analog at that level. Shutter ladder 1/50–1/4000 (6 stops), so two laps with the lamp dimmed ~6 stops in between.
- Result, camera .11, 2026-11-06, DC lamp only, references luma 105.7 (lap 1) and 114.4 (lap 2) (tools/iso_calibration/data/…_ref106.csv), (A, D): ISO 100 (0,0), 200 (11,0), 400 (20,0), 800 (40,0), 1600 (83,0), 3200 (167,0), 6400 (179,8), 12800 (191,16), 25600 (235,28), 51200 (252,56), 102400 (255,104), 204800 (255,160). ISO 409600 out of reach (full gain ~0.15 stop short). Low stops agree with the earlier 4K runs (0/11/20/40/84–87).
- Stop accuracy: each stop's brightness matched within ±1 % from ISO 800 up, −3.3 %/+2.7 % at ISO 200/400 (coarse low analog steps).
- Gains depend on the reference brightness (ISO 800: 40 at luma 106–123, 43 at 113, 51 at 87, 55 at 37), so references must be mid-gray (the tool enforces luma 100–140). Daylight drifted 0.7 stop in 30 min during one run, so calibrate under a DC lamp alone.
- ISO labels are relative ("ISO 100" = no gain). Datasheet-derived absolute base ISO (saturation-based, ISO 12232: 78/(3895/sensitivity)) ≈ 300–330 at 0 dB [INFERRED] — unconfirmed: sensor conversion-gain mode, whether camera gain 0 = sensor 0 dB, and SOS vs saturation-based definition could each shift it ≥1 stop. Confirm with a light meter at f/1.5 (wide end).

## Low light [MEASURED where marked, else INFERRED]
Brightness levers, biggest first, for a dim scene:
1. Auto exposure: raise max_sys_gain from the default 12 (~ISO 29k ceiling) to ≥96 (full gain; PRO `max_iso` 409600) [MEASURED: luma 13 → 118 with the shutter limited to 1/4000], with auto_AGain_max and auto_DGain_max at 255 (lower values cap it first, see above); raising blc_level brightens the auto-exposure target further. Costs noise; it is the one setting that makes auto exposure use the sensor's ~71 dB of gain.
2. Longer exposure: at 30 fps the longest is ~1/30 s. Halving the frame rate to 15 fps allows ~1/15 s, ~+1 stop [MEASURED]; slow shutter (low_farme_rate) lets auto exposure go to 1/20 s at 21 fps by itself: half the noise, but not brighter (see above) [MEASURED]. Costs motion blur and frame rate, worse on a moving vehicle.
3. Zoom to the wide end: F1.5 at 3.6 mm; the tele f-number is unknown (measure it: the same scene at both ends, ISO fixed).
4. Manual exposure: PRO Manual reaches ISO 204800 (A=255 D=160) regardless of max_sys_gain [MEASURED], but only with auto_AGain_max and auto_DGain_max open (≥ 84 and ≥ 40).
5. Light: the module has IR/white-light outputs (led_control, led_control_avail=1); whether this product wires any lamp to them is unconfirmed. Otherwise a vehicle light is the real fix.

Image quality at high gain (not measured; needs a noise metric, not just mean luma):
- 3D noise reduction (noiseReduction) and 2D NR (_2DNR_level): no effect; the firmware never applies them (see Firmware knob map). Noise reduction strength follows ISO only.
- color_black (black-and-white) drops chroma noise.
- Noise eats bitrate: at high gain raise the main stream's bitrate or use H.265, or the encoder turns noise into blocks.
- The ~1.5 % creep after every setting change hints at a slow adaptive process (maybe the unknown "low-light enhancement"); find it before relying on fine exposure control.
